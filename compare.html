The honest version, one by one

DiveShop360

Good at: retail. If your shop is mostly a store — selling gear, barcodes, stock on shelves — their POS heritage shows, and they do service work orders too.

The difference: it starts at roughly twice DiveTower's price, top plans carry a setup fee of up to $3,000, and it's built around the counter — not the boat, the pickups, the instructors' day, or the student's phone. It also needs a constant internet connection, which islands don't always offer.

Bloowatch

Good at: multi-channel bookings for watersports schools in general — surf, kite, sail, and dive.

The difference: "in general" is the point. DiveTower only knows dive shops — certifications, dive-by-dive progress, gear sizes, cylinder inspections, agency paperwork. And Bloowatch requires a 12-month contract; DiveTower is monthly, stop any time.

EVE

Good at: long-established, well known in the industry, strong for dive resorts with accommodation.

The difference: a different generation of software. DiveTower was born on phones — every staff member, every customer, works from the device already in their pocket, with nothing to install. And you see our price on the website; no sales call needed.

DiverDash

Good at: a friendly, affordable starting point — scheduling, customers, and equipment tracking from $39/month.

The difference: the $39 plan is the starting point, and features grow with the price. DiveTower has no ladder to climb: $99 is everything — bookings, all staff apps, the customer app with videos and paperwork, accounting, equipment — for every shop, from day one.

FareHarbor & general booking platforms

Good at: online bookings for any tour or activity, with no monthly subscription.

The difference: "no subscription" means a fee added to every single online booking instead — busy shops can pay far more than $99/month without noticing. And a booking widget is where DiveTower starts, not where it ends: it doesn't run your gear room, your salaries, your pickups, your paperwork, or your accounting.
